# Virtue Wheel Prompts — Aristotle's Twelve, in Two Registers (System B)

The second of the four new Dozens (owner-sealed 2026-07-22, RESTRUCTURE
§New Content). **System B — the month-aligned wheel** (crown, root, six
opposition axes; see [The DOMY Canon](../../../CANON.md), §The Two Dozen
Systems), carrying **Aristotle's twelve moral virtues** and their
twelve failing vices as **TWO REGISTERS OF ONE WHEEL** — the
paint/light doctrine made a Dozen. The LIGHT register draws the twelve
virtues; the PAINT register draws the twelve vices. Light is additive —
a virtue is made of the light that passes THROUGH the glass; paint is
subtractive — a vice is heavy pigment that blocks it. Same twelve
seats, two ways of reading the same wheel.

**The twelve pairs (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II–IV):**
Courage/Cowardice, Temperance/Gluttony, Generosity/Greed,
Magnificence/Vulgarity, Magnanimity/Vanity, Patience/Wrath,
Truthfulness/Boastfulness, Wit/Buffoonery, Friendliness/Flattery,
Modesty/Shamelessness, Right Ambition/Over-ambition, Just
Indignation/Envy. Each virtue is a MEAN; each vice its extreme.

**The seating — RE-SEATED AND SEALED by the owner 2026-07-29** (CANON
§The Virtue Wheel). **A virtue and its vice share ONE seat:** the same
hour read two ways, light or paint, perfectly co-located. Two things
died with the seal — Courage no longer crowns (the owner's ruling), and
the old "paint-is-light-inverted-through-the-center" proposal, which
put each vice diametrically opposite its own virtue, is GONE.

| Hour | Virtue (light) | Vice (paint) | Why this seat |
|---|---|---|---|

**The six axes:** 12↔24 what I deserve ↔ what OTHERS deserve (on the
paint, Vanity ↔ Envy); 14↔02 measure in WORD ↔ measure in APPETITE;
16↔04 fortitude's two acts, ATTACK ↔ ENDURE (Aquinas, ST II-II q.123:
*aggredi* and *sustinere*); 18↔06 claiming one's due ↔ declining the
undue; 20↔08 the GREAT gift ↔ the DAILY gift (*megaloprepeia* and
*eleutheriotes*); 22↔10 the two graces of company, PLAY ↔ KINDNESS.
The briefs below are unchanged by the seating — each figure is argued
by its own nature, hue following that nature.

**Register:** the round calendar-mount medallion family — the same
night-window stained-glass rondel every Calendar-pointer 12-set wears
(see the [Slavic Months Prompts](../months/months_prompts.md), the
[Calendar Prompts](../archetype/calendar_prompts.md)). ROUND rose-window
medallions, one unifying device across all twenty-six: **the Mean** —
a slender balance-beam leadwork rim laid across the top of the disc. On
a VIRTUE the beam hangs LEVEL and true, cast in bright clear lead; on a
VICE the beam is TILTED hard to one side (its own excess or deficiency)
and cast in tarnished, corroded lead. The border itself tells virtue
from vice at a glance, the way the Prism-light pillars carry their own
shadow.

## The center — Prudence & Cunning (the axle, both registers, always present)

The thirteenth takes no wedge: it stands at the CENTER, the one member
that does not turn with the twelve — **the wheel turns on it** (CANON
§The Axle). This Dozen is two registers of one wheel, so its axle has
two faces: the same skill, driven two ways. Both are ALWAYS present —
person-centers never leave, unlike the calendar-driven thirteenths that
appear only when the year overflows. (Folly, the mere absence of
judgment, was weighed and set aside: the axle must STEER. Folly
sleeps.)

**Prudence — the charioteer (LIGHT register)** → `assets/calendars/virtues/primary/colored/Prudence.png`

*(Axle note: PRUDENCE — Aristotle's *phronesis* — is the judgment that
FINDS every other virtue's mean. Without it courage turns reckless and
generosity wasteful; it is not one virtue among twelve but the one that
seats the twelve. The medievals named it* auriga virtutum, *the
charioteer of the virtues — and a charioteer steers from the center,
never rides the rim.)*

```
ROUND rose-window stained-glass medallion, night-window register, photorealistic render, isolated background, the circular window shape IS the frame, made of LIGHT passing through — luminous, additive, no heavy pigment. Clear gold and warm daylight glass, the steadiest and most even light of the twenty-six, no glare and no shadow. Center: a calm charioteer seated dead-center of the disc holding the reins of a team that circles AROUND him, both hands level, eyes on the road ahead; a small clear mirror at his belt (seeing things as they are) and a serpent coiled at his feet held in check, the twelve spokes of a wheel running out from him to the rim — the judgment that steers from the middle and never rides the rim. Everything composed concentrically around his stillness. Border: the shared Mean leadwork rim, the balance-beam hanging LEVEL and true across the top, cast in bright clear lead. Palette: clear gold, warm daylight, level white light, bright clear lead. NO lettering anywhere.
```

**Cunning — the dark charioteer (PAINT register)** → `assets/calendars/vices/primary/colored/Cunning.png`

*(Axle note: CUNNING — Aristotle's own* panourgia, *cleverness in the
service of base ends (EN VI.12) — is the same skill driving the
opposite way. Aquinas seats* astutia *as the vice that COUNTERFEITS
prudence (ST II-II q.55);* "now the serpent was more cunning than any
beast of the field" *(Genesis 3:1). The dark charioteer does not miss
the road — he CHOOSES the cliff.)*

```
ROUND rose-window stained-glass medallion, night-window register, photorealistic render, isolated background, the circular window shape IS the frame, made of heavy PAINT that BLOCKS the light — thick, opaque, subtractive pigment. Oily green-black and cold tarnished-silver glass, clever and lightless. Center: a hooded charioteer seated dead-center holding the reins with total control and deliberately turning the team toward a broken cliff-edge at the rim, the road behind him whole and abandoned; a great serpent coiled up his arm and whispering at his ear, a crooked mirror at his belt showing a flattering lie, the same twelve spokes running out from him but each one bent — mastery of the means with the end chosen wrong. Everything composed concentrically, calm and unhurried, which is the horror of it. Border: the shared Mean leadwork rim, the beam TILTED hard to one side, cast in tarnished corroded lead. Palette: oily green-black, tarnished silver, cold shadow, corroded lead. NO lettering anywhere.
```
