## 1. Crown-text hovers

*(the new law: every crown text explains ITSELF, never the letter under it —
[ring_rework.md](ring_rework.md) §3)*

**ANNUIT COEPTIS** — "He has favored our undertakings." The shorter half of
the Great Seal's reverse motto, adapted from Virgil's *Aeneid* (IX.625,
*audacibus annue coeptis* — "look with favor on this bold endeavor"): a
prayer, not a boast — the Eye of Providence is asked to bless the work, the
work does not claim to have earned it on its own.

**NOVUS ORDO SECLORUM** — "A new order of the ages." The longer half of the
same motto, drawn from Virgil's Fourth Eclogue (*magnus ab integro saeclorum
nascitur ordo* — "the great line of the centuries begins anew"): not a new
order built to replace the old by force, but a new age understood to have
already begun — the pyramid's unfinished top, a work still under
construction.

**FEAR** — the dark path's first station (DOMY, 20h): the panic that seizes
before the mind can weigh the danger, Ysteria's own root — the body deciding
before the will does. "The thing which I greatly feared is come upon me"
(Job 3:25).

**ANGER** — the dark path's second station (DOMY, 24h): wrath kept past
sundown, the LIVID hour — *lividus*, Latin for bruise-purple, the color rage
leaves on the skin and on the night. "Let not the sun go down upon your
wrath" (Ephesians 4:26) — this station is what happens when it does.

**HATE** — the dark path's third station (DOMY, 4h): anger gone cold and
decided, no longer a flare but a verdict held before dawn. "He that hateth
his brother is in darkness" (1 John 2:11) — the hour before light is where
the verdict is signed.

**SUFFERING** — the dark path's fourth and last station (DOMY, 12h): what
fermented all night stands exposed in full noon light. "Nothing is secret,
that shall not be made manifest" (Luke 8:17) — the dark path does not end in
relief, it ends SEEN.

**HOPE** — the light path's first station (LOOP, 8h): the good heart that
dares the morning before it has any evidence to dare on — Tharsos, courage
as a seed, not yet a deed. "Be of good cheer" — *tharseite* (John 16:33).

**FAITH** — the light path's second station (LOOP, 12h): not belief that a
thing is true but service to whom it is owed — Latria, the whole liturgy of
loyalty held at noon, in full light, where it costs the most. "Faith is the
substance of things hoped for" (Hebrews 11:1).

**LOVE** — the light path's third station (LOOP, 16h): the longing that
moves the hand, Pothos turned into an act — not the feeling but what the
feeling makes you do by evening. "Greater love hath no man than this" (John
15:13).

**SALVATION** — the light path's fourth and last station (LOOP, 24h): the
help that delivers, arriving at the identical midnight hour DOMY's Anger
keeps — the same clock face, the opposite outcome. "At midnight Paul and
Silas prayed… and all the doors were opened" (Acts 16:25–26).

**NON NOBIS DOMINE** — "Not unto us, O Lord." The opening of Psalm 115:1
(*Non nobis, Domine, non nobis, sed nomini tuo da gloriam* — "not unto us,
but unto thy name, give glory") and the Knights Templar's own battle motto:
a vow of no personal credit sworn by men who fought and died for it — the
order's whole ethic compressed into five words borrowed from a psalm older
than the order by two thousand years.

**ΙΧΘΥΣ** — the fish. A Greek acrostic, not a word chosen for its shape:
Ἰησοῦς Χριστὸς Θεοῦ Υἱὸς Σωτήρ, "Jesus Christ, Son of God, Savior" — each of
the five letters opens one clause of the confession, and only when read
together do they also spell the fish early Christians drew in secret as a
recognition mark.

**IN HOC SIGNO VINCES** — "In this sign, conquer." The words Constantine
reported seeing beside a cross of light before the Battle of the Milvian
Bridge (Eusebius, *Life of Constantine* I.28) — THIS sign being the Chi-Rho,
the first two Greek letters of Χριστός overlaid; the labarum built from the
vision carried the sentence into battle and, after the victory, into the
empire's own standard.

**The live time crown, The One** — "the civil hour of your own city": the
top arc reads whatever a clock on your own wall would read, in your own
timezone — The One is the hour ring, so its live text is the plainest
possible statement of its doctrine: the wheel telling you what hour it
already is.

**The One's location line** — the bottom arc names the city and country the
watch is set to: the counterpart to the live hour above it — WHERE anchors
WHEN, the two lines that make a clock a fact about a specific place rather
than an abstraction.

**The live time crown, Templar** — "the hour of Jerusalem, the city of the
vow": the top arc keeps a second zone regardless of where the watch itself
sits — a knight's watch was always measured against the city his order swore
to, not against wherever he was standing.

## 2. Alphabet-ordinal hover lines

### DOMY (M · Y · Ω · D)

- **M, 12h** — M is the 13th letter of the modern Latin alphabet, no relation
  to its noon seat; it stands here for Miseria, not for its ordinal.
- **Y, 20h** — Y is the 25th letter of the modern Latin alphabet (22nd of the
  23-letter classical Latin row that omits J, U, W) — neither number is 20;
  Y sits at 20h for Ysteria's initial, not for any ordinal claim.
- **Ω, 24h** — Ω is the 24th and last letter of the Greek alphabet, seated
  exactly on the 24th hour — the one true ordinal match on this card, shared
  with LOOP's own Ω.
- **D, 4h** — D is the 4th letter of the modern Latin alphabet, seated
  exactly on the 4th hour — its ordinal and its station word (Detestatio)
  agree by the same coincidence that seats M elsewhere on this card.

### LOOP (L · Π · Ω · Θ)

- **L, 12h** — L is the 12th letter of the modern Latin alphabet, seated
  exactly on the 12th hour/noon — an ordinal match; PILOT's alphabetical-hour
  cipher held for this letter even though it broke for the card as a whole
  (Λ, the Greek equivalent, is the 11th letter, not the 12th).
- **Π, 16h** — Π is the 16th letter of the Greek alphabet, seated exactly on
  the 16th hour — an ordinal match.
- **Ω, 24h** — Ω is the 24th and last letter of the Greek alphabet, seated
  exactly on the 24th hour — the same match DOMY's Ω carries.
- **Θ, 8h** — Θ is the 8th letter of the Greek alphabet, seated exactly on
  the 8th hour — an ordinal match.

### The One (Ω, on `octa`)

- **Ω, 24h** — Ω is the 24th and last letter of the Greek alphabet, seated
  exactly on the 24th hour where the day's count would otherwise run out of
  numbers — the one glyph on this card that is not a number, closing the
  alphabet where the clock closes the day.
- **12 / 15 / 18 / 21 / 3 / 6 / 9** — no ordinal line: these seven seats wear
  the hour itself as the glyph, so the numeral and its "ordinal" are the same
  fact stated once; an ordinal sentence here would just repeat the station
  name.

### Dollar (👁 · S · M · Ω · N · A)

- **👁, 12h** — no ordinal line: the Eye of Providence is a pictogram, not a
  letter, and carries no position in any alphabet to compare against noon.
- **S, 16h** — S is the 19th letter of the modern Latin alphabet, no relation
  to its 16h seat; it stands here for Sigma/Satanic Scourge, not for its
  ordinal.
- **M, 20h** — M is the 13th letter of the modern Latin alphabet, no
  relation to its 20h seat; it stands here for Master/Malignant Accuser, not
  for its ordinal.
- **Ω, 24h** — Ω is the 24th and last letter of the Greek alphabet, seated
  exactly on the 24th hour — the same match every other Ω seat on this dial
  carries.
- **N, 4h** — N is the 14th letter of the modern Latin alphabet, no relation
  to its 4h seat; it stands here for Nazarene, not for its ordinal.
- **A, 8h** — A is the 1st letter of the modern Latin alphabet (and of the
  Greek), no relation to its 8h seat; it stands here for Alpha/Aegis, not for
  its ordinal.

### The One (Ω, live-hour crown card) / Templar (✠) / CHI (X)

- **Templar, ✠ at 12h/18h/24h/6h** — no ordinal line: the cross pattée is a
  symbol, not a letter, and repeats unchanged at all four seats — the
  card's whole doctrine is that the glyph does NOT vary, only the station
  does, so an ordinal comparison has nothing to attach to.
- **CHI, X at 24h** — X is the 24th letter of the modern Latin alphabet,
  seated exactly on the 24th hour — the Latin twin of Ω's own match on the
  identical seat, on a different card.

## 3. GUIDE passages — the two world-modes

*([ring_rework.md](ring_rework.md) §1)*

**GEOCENTRIC (Ptolemy)**

Welcome to the oldest way of telling time: the way it feels. Stand still and
watch the sky, and the sun is the one that moves — that was Ptolemy's
picture in the *Almagest*, the observer fixed at the center, every light
overhead traveling around him. This dial draws exactly that feeling: the
hour band stays put, twelve always on top, and the star chases true solar
noon across a face that never turns. Nothing here is wrong to see this way —
it is how the sky actually looks from where you are standing, every single
day. Choose Geocentric when you want the dial to hold still and let the sun
do the walking, the way every sundial before Copernicus ever did.

**HELIOCENTRIC (Copernicus)**

Now try the harder truth to feel: you are the one moving. Copernicus argued
it in *De revolutionibus* — the sun holds still, and it is the world,
carrying you, that turns beneath it. This dial draws that instead: the sun
is pinned at the top, the outer band turns to keep true noon there, and the
star stands upright while the ground turns under it. At dusk and dawn the
whole face turns over — a short, deliberate flip, not a glitch — because
when the world you are standing on rolls into its own shadow, what belongs
on top changes too. Choose Heliocentric when you want the dial to show you
the turning you cannot otherwise feel.

## 4. The CHI article draft

*(built from [ring_rework.md](ring_rework.md) §4's dossier — 300–450 words)*

Plato, in the *Timaeus* (36b), tells how the Maker of the world took the
strip of the World-Soul and split it lengthwise into two bands, then bent
each into a circle and crossed them "in the shape of the letter Χ" — one
band became the celestial equator, the other the ecliptic, the sun's own
path tilted against it. Where the two circles cross is not a detail of the
geometry; it is the whole point of it. Those two crossings are the
EQUINOXES — the only two moments in the year when day and night divide
evenly — and this dial pins them at exactly 90° and 270°, the letter Χ
literally drawn across its own year wheel by Plato's own construction,
twenty-four centuries before this instrument existed.

The horizon draws a second Χ of its own. East and west sit at 6h and 18h,
the two gates the sun uses on an equal day; move to the solstices and the
sunrise and sunset points slide apart into four positions that, connected,
trace a saltire across the compass rose — the same cross the hexagram
pointer's own extreme rays already draw, this time written by the horizon
instead of by the sky above it.

Χ is the 24th and final letter of the classical Greek alphabet — the Latin
alphabet's own 24th letter, X, inherits the seat, so on the 24th hour this
card seats the Latin twin of the Ω every other preset already keeps there.
It is also the initial of Χριστός, Christ — the letter at the heart of the
Chi-Rho, the labarum Constantine carried under the sentence IN HOC SIGNO
VINCES; it sits inside ΙΧΘΥΣ, the fish acrostic, as the second letter of the
confession it spells; and it still opens "Xmas" today, the abbreviation a
straight descendant of the monogram, not a modern shortcut.

Outside scripture the letter keeps working as a mark of crossing and of the
unknown: St Andrew's saltire flies the same shape as a flag; algebra reaches
for X as the value no number has yet named — fittingly, for the one hour on
this dial that no number can say either, since midnight closes one day and
opens the next rather than merely counting up to it. It is the unlettered
signer's own mark, the treasure map's X, the crossroads where two paths
meet and one must be chosen. And in Rome, X was simply ten — the number
behind the Decalogue, the ten commandments handed down as law, a second
quiet echo of a cross built from a count.

**A · DOMY** — "Domini — the Lord's, as in Anno Domini: every date the world
writes is counted as the Year of the Lord, and this dial wears that name.
DOMY is the dark watch of the pair: the shadowed cross of Fear, Anger, Hate
and Suffering, walked through the night so the day can be walked free. The
name is the word Domini bent into a wheel — the Lord's own hours, kept even
in the dark."

**B · LOOP** — "The loop has no end — that is its whole theology.
Twenty-four hours joined in a ring; Omega, the letter of endings, bent here
into O, a circle that cannot end; the light cross of Hope, Faith, Love and
Salvation, whose last station at midnight hands the wheel back to the first
at dawn. Read the four seats aloud and they say the name: L-Ω-Ω-Π — LOOP,
the day without end, world without end."

**C · Dollar** — "The Great Seal of the one-dollar bill, laid on the wheel:
the Eye of Providence above the crown, the mottos of the unfinished pyramid
arcing outside. The most-held object on Earth carries the oldest symbols of
the newest empire — and between Judge and Creator, Accuser and Advocate,
this dial spells what the bill only hints."

**D · The One** — "The denomination itself: E PLURIBUS UNUM — out of many,
one. Out of twenty-four hours, one wheel; out of seven numbered seats, one
letter — Ω at midnight, where the count ends and the day is one again. The
dial stripped to pure count, and the count's one exception; its crown keeps
your own hour and your own city."

**E · Templar** — "The order founded to guard Jerusalem still keeps its
watch here: four crosses, four watches — noon, evening, midnight, morning —
the same vigil under every light, the Beauceant's black-over-white worn as a
day and a night. The crown ticks the hour of Jerusalem, the city of the vow,
under the order's own motto: NON NOBIS DOMINE — not unto us, O Lord, but
unto Thy name give glory."

**F · CHI** — "Chi — the letter the cosmos is written in. Plato taught that
the Maker crossed the two great circles of heaven 'in the shape of the
letter Χ' — and every equinox redraws that X where the sun's road crosses
the sky's equator. On the horizon the four solstice suns rise and set on its
arms; on the wheel the 24th letter stands alone at the 24th hour: the
initial of Christós, the sign in which Constantine conquered, the saltire of
the first-called apostle, the unknown with which every unlettered soul still
signs."
